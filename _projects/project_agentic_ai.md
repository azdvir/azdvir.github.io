The rise of **Agentic AI** — systems in which multiple autonomous AI agents collaborate, delegate tasks, and communicate over networks — introduces a fundamentally new attack surface. Unlike traditional software, agents make autonomous decisions, interact with external tools and APIs, and may operate across organizational boundaries, making their communications a critical security concern.

This project addresses the security challenges unique to agentic AI architectures:

**Key research questions:**
- How can agents authenticate each other and establish trust without centralized authority?
- What are the attack vectors specific to agent-to-agent communication protocols?
- How can we detect prompt injection, goal hijacking, and adversarial manipulation in multi-agent pipelines?

**Our approach:**
- Developing **cryptographic attestation** mechanisms for agent identity and provenance
- Designing **anomaly detection** models trained on agent communication patterns
- Building **sandboxing and monitoring** frameworks for agentic systems deployed in sensitive environments (healthcare, finance, critical infrastructure)

This research sits at the intersection of network security, AI safety, and distributed systems — positioning Ariel University at the forefront of securing the next generation of AI deployments.
